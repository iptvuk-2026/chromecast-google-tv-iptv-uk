Setting Up IPTV on Chromecast with Google TV: A Complete UK Guide

Chromecast with Google TV is one of the easiest ways to watch IPTV in the UK. It has its own home screen, remote and Google Play Store, so you install an IPTV player on the device itself, log in once, and watch from the sofa. This guide covers the whole job, from plugging it in to fixing first-evening faults.

Which Chromecast Do You Have? It Changes Everything

Google has sold several devices called Chromecast, and they do not all work the same way. The one this guide is about is Chromecast with Google TV, the small oval stick with a voice remote, sold in 4K and cheaper HD versions. It runs Google TV, Google’s newer interface on top of Android TV, and installs apps directly from Google Play.

Older Chromecast dongles have no remote or apps: they only show what you cast from a phone. Google has since moved on to the Google TV Streamer, and the same steps work on it too. If you only want to know what IPTV is first, that guide explains it.

Before You Start: Five Quick Checks

First, you need a Google account: Google TV will not finish setting up without one, so use the account you want to keep. Second, check your Wi-Fi: the Chromecast needs the same network your phone is on during setup, with a strong signal at the television. If streams stutter later, our guide to IPTV internet speed explains what speed you need.

Third, have your IPTV login ready: the server address, username and password your provider sent you. Fourth, you need a free HDMI port and a spare power socket, because the Chromecast needs its own power cable. Fifth, put the batteries in the remote and keep your phone nearby with Bluetooth on.

Setting Up the Chromecast, Step by Step

Follow these in order. Each one is short, and the app tells you what to do next.

  1. Plug it in. Push the Chromecast into a free HDMI port, connect the power cable to a wall socket, and switch the television to that HDMI input.
  2. Pair the remote. Put the batteries in the voice remote. It should pair within a few seconds; if not, hold Back and Home together until the light blinks.
  3. Open Google Home. Install the Google Home app on your phone if you do not have it, open it, and sign in with the Google account you want on the television.
  4. Add the device. In Google Home, tap the plus sign, choose Set up device, then New devices, and pick the home you want it in. The app finds the Chromecast over Bluetooth.
  5. Connect to Wi-Fi. Choose your home Wi-Fi network and enter the password. The Chromecast joins the network, checks for updates, and may restart once.
  6. Finish on the television. Confirm your Google account, skip the apps you do not want, and wait for the Google TV home screen, with its Apps tab and search bar.

Installing an IPTV Player from Google Play

This step makes Google TV easier than a Firestick. There is no Downloader app, no developer-options toggle and no file to install by hand. You open the Play Store on the television, search for the player by name, and press Install, exactly as you would on a phone. Our full IPTV apps library compares the players.

From the home screen, go to the Apps tab, choose Search for apps, and type the player name, or hold the Assistant button and say it aloud. Open the result, check the developer name, and press Install. When it finishes, press Open.

Logging In: Xtream Codes or a Playlist

Most players offer two ways in, and Xtream Codes is the one to choose. Open the player, choose Add playlist, then Xtream Codes, and enter the server address exactly as your provider sent it, then your username and password. The player pulls in your channels, films, series and programme guide, usually within a minute. Our Xtream Codes guide explains the method, and the M3U playlist guide covers the other way in.

The other way in is an M3U playlist link, which gives a plainer guide and, on most players, no catch-up. Use Xtream Codes if you have both. Take care with the server address: one wrong character and the login fails with no useful error. Copy it exactly, including the port number after the colon, and do not add spaces. If the guide looks empty, read our EPG guide and the fix for an EPG that is not updating. If channels will not load, the fix for channels not loading covers the next fault.

Older Chromecasts: Casting from Your Phone Instead

If your Chromecast is an older dongle with no remote and no home screen, you cannot install a player on it, because it has no apps of its own. Install the player on your Android phone from Google Play, log in there with the same Xtream Codes details, and use the cast button inside the player, if it offers one, to send the picture to the television. Keep the phone on the same Wi-Fi network and charged.

Casting works, but it is not as good as running the player on the television: a session drops when the phone rings or the Wi-Fi wobbles, and you get no television guide on the big screen. The smart TV guide is worth reading if your television already has Google TV built in.

Picture, Sound and Speed on a Small Stick

The Chromecast has two limits to plan around: storage and Wi-Fi. Storage is only a few gigabytes once the system has taken its share, so install one player, not five, and delete apps you do not use. If the device says it is full, go to Settings, then Apps, and clear the cache of the apps you use most. The 4K model plays 4K streams when your subscription and television both support them; the HD model tops out at high definition.

Wi-Fi is the other limit. The Chromecast has no ethernet port, so it relies on your wireless network, and a weak signal at the television is the most common cause of buffering. Use the 5 GHz network if the television is in the same room as the router, and the 2.4 GHz network if there are walls in between, because it travels further. A USB-C hub with an ethernet port is the most reliable fix for evening buffering. Our guides to IPTV buffering, IPTV freezing and IPTV not working cover the rest.

Troubleshooting: The Faults Everyone Hits First

First, Google Home cannot find the Chromecast. Move your phone closer, switch Bluetooth on, and check both are on the same Wi-Fi network. Second, the television shows no signal. You are on the wrong HDMI input, or the power cable is in a television USB port that does not supply enough power: use a wall socket. Third, the player installs but channels will not load. That is a login problem, not a Chromecast problem: re-enter the server address, username and password, check your subscription is active, and read the fix for IPTV login problems.

Fourth, the picture buffers every evening. That is usually Wi-Fi or speed, not the player: test your speed at the television, move closer to the router or use a wired adapter, and read our buffering guide. Fifth, the guide is empty: give it a few minutes on first login, then refresh it, and read the EPG guide if it stays empty.

Staying Safe on Google TV

Google TV is safe when you use it as intended: install players from the Play Store, log in with a subscription you pay for, and keep the device updated. The risk is never the Chromecast itself; it is the file or the service you choose. Only install a player from Google Play, never from a site you do not recognise. Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to sideload anything on Chromecast with Google TV?

No. It runs Google TV and has the full Google Play Store, so TiviMate, IPTV Smarters Pro and the other players install directly. Sideloading is a Firestick workaround, not something this device needs.

Can I watch IPTV on an older Chromecast with no remote?

Yes, by casting from your phone: install the player on your phone, log in, and use the cast button if the player offers one. It works, but it is less reliable than running the player on the television.

Which player should I install first?

TiviMate, if you want the best television guide, or IPTV Smarters Pro, if you want the easiest first setup. Both install from Google Play and both use the same Xtream Codes login.

Does Chromecast with Google TV support 4K IPTV?

The 4K model does, when your subscription and television both support 4K. The HD model tops out at high definition. Either way, you need a fast, stable connection for the best picture.

Chromecast with Google TV earns its place as the easiest way to watch IPTV on a television, because it removes the one awkward step every other small stick has: there is nothing to sideload. Plug it in, set it up with the Google Home app, install one player from Google Play, log in with Xtream Codes, and you are watching. The remote and voice search make it feel like a set-top box, and updates take care of themselves.
